# Related work

Other acroyoga references and tools, as found during the synthetic user research (`docs/synthetic-users/`), checked 2026-10. Most are video-first libraries; none is a map of which poses connect, and none lets someone compose their own flow and share it as one link. That is where this site differs, not in completeness.

## Libraries
- **AcroPassport** (https://acropassport.com/skills-library): about 1,559 community-built skills (poses, transitions, sequences) across L-basing, standing, 3-high and more, with alternate names, difficulty sort and video links; free to browse, logging own skills needs an account and a vouch. The largest reference.
- **Jacob Brown** (https://jacobbrownacro.com/, https://www.jacobbrownacro.com/washing-machines): "Machines A–Z" with 1,200+ washing machines plus standing, icarian and whips, every entry a credited video, updated weekly; no difficulty, no descriptions. The usual answer to "what is this called?".
- **acroyogavideos.com** (https://acroyogavideos.com): about 7,776 videos with category, level and channel filters.
- **Acrodemy** (https://acrodemy.eu/acroyoga-poses/, https://acrodemy.eu/sequences-flows-washing-machines/): poses and flows grouped by level and family, links easier to harder versions.
- **AcroAdventure** (https://acroadventure.com/library/flows/): flows named by their poses, with level codes.
- **YogaSlackers** (https://yogaslackers.com/explore/acro/library/): a few dozen videos without levels.
- **Partner Acrobatics manual** (https://partneracrobatics.com/manual/): a long reference list; shorthand, duplicates and inconsistent names.
- **JoinTheCirc.us**: a flow index some call their favourite, others "not super useful or usable".
- **Acropedia**: the classic reference, unreachable (HTTP 522) in 2026-10. **Acromaps**, an earlier map, is gone too; people ask of any new site whether it will survive.
- Pose names and teaching material from **Christian Gieger** (https://www.christiangieger.de/acroyoga-poses/) and **yogaemotion** (https://www.yogaemotion.net/courses), see `ROADMAP.md` (other sources).

## Apps
- **AcroFlow** (https://acroflow.app/): pre-launch with a waitlist; "movement map" of connected transitions, flow building with notes, practice-path generator, for personal notes rather than coaching. The closest to this site's direction.
- **Acro Yoga Flow** (iOS, https://apps.apple.com/ph/app/acro-yoga-flow/id6466607589): moves library, progress tracking, partner map; barely rated.
- **Acrodicted** (iOS): 140+ poses and 380+ transitions; last updated 2018.
- **Simon Says: Acro!** (Android): calls out poses at a jam; users asked for levels per pose and toggling poses.

## Other formats
- **Moonkey Pills** (https://www.verkami.com/projects/30975-moonkey-pills-acroyoga-cards-englishspanish-version): a 60-card pose deck, made because students could not match names to shapes.
- Laminated one-page cheat sheets made by teachers for their classes.
- Paid curricula on Skool communities (weekly lesson videos, "searchable pose database" plus courses) and studio apps with individual learning paths, e.g. https://learninglab.fans/acroyoga/.
